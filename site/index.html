    When many AI agents work together, a few compromised members can steer the whole group to a wrong answer. This project borrows coordination rules from ant trails, honeybee nest-site choice, wolf packs, fireflies and bird flocks, gives each rule to a swarm of language-model agents, and plants compromised agents that push a wrong "target" option. Every honest agent holds only a few facts, so the group has to pool evidence to find the right answer. We measure how far the compromise spreads (contamination), how often honest agents still choose correctly (accuracy), the attacker share at which a swarm tips over, and which defences (evaporation, stop signals, quorum, dilution, model diversity, sparse wiring) buy the most safety for the least loss of usefulness. The design and hypotheses were pre-registered before any experiment ran.

    Contamination
    Share of honest agents whose final choice is the attackers' target. Lower is better.
    Accuracy
    Share of honest agents whose final choice is correct. Reported at f = 0 (no attackers).
    f
    Fraction of agents that are compromised.
    f*
    Tipping point: the f at which a logistic fit of contamination crosses 0.5.
    CI
    95% bootstrap confidence interval over seeds, shown as whiskers.
